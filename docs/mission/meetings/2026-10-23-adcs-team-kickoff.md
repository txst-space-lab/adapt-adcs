## Team scope (lead's initial view)

- ADCS is responsible for maneuvering the ADAPT spacecraft. The lead's role is to keep the team unblocked: find resources and information, and connect people.
- The team will likely choose the reaction wheels and own the flight software that manages them. F Prime (F´) is the likely software foundation.
- ADAPT leadership had worked on the mission over the summer; the ADCS team hadn't synced with them yet.

## Interface with Payload

- From a Payload team meeting: there will be two computers on board. The payload computer runs the ML model, and ADCS won't have access to it. It will send commands to ADCS, and Payload will want to control the reaction wheels (or whatever actuators fly).
- ADCS will work closely with Payload. Team members are encouraged to talk to the Payload team and attend some of their meetings.

## Mission background

- ADAPT started as a mission concept the lab proposed to the University Nanosatellite Program (UNP). After it was selected, three undergraduates (now the project leadership) attended UNP training in Albuquerque.
- Recordings of the UNP workshops have been shared with the team. They are long and technical, so plan to sit down and watch them properly.

## Reaction wheels

- OreSat at Portland State built their own reaction wheels from drone motors. These are probably not accurate or reliable enough for ADAPT's flight needs. If a large funding round comes through, the mission would more likely buy a COTS unit (e.g. Blue Canyon).
- A lab-built set might cost a couple hundred dollars. It would be good for prototyping, especially for the software. OreSat's software is open source.

## Collaboration

- The team will share data and calculations through Git. Everyone should attend the lab's GitHub workshop. Texas State gives free access to O'Reilly books, which include a short hands-on introduction to Git.
